## Are there consistent tiers (budget vs premium resellers) and what differentiates them?

### Takeaway
Yes — three rough tiers emerge: (1) NZD-native, COA-on-request storefronts positioning themselves as "trusted NZ suppliers" with fast domestic shipping and structured bulk discounts; (2) budget-undercutting NZ storefronts competing mainly on sticker price; and (3) an informal social-media/DM "black market" tier that is actually the **most expensive and least verifiable**, contradicting the usual assumption that black-market channels are cheap.

### Cited Findings
- New Zealand Peptides differentiates on **bulk/loyalty discounting**: 5% off buying 2, 10% off buying 3, 15% off buying 4+ (e.g., Tirzepatide 10mg drops from $150 → $142.50 → $135.00 → $127.50 per vial across those tiers), plus GST-inclusive pricing and "no card-processor surcharge" as a trust signal — [New Zealand Peptides tirzepatide product](https://newzealandpeptides.co.nz/product/tirzepatide/)
- New Zealand Peptides and NZ Peptide (nzpeptide.com) both foreground **COA/testing claims** as a differentiator: "HPLC-MS verified" and "99%+ purity" (New Zealand Peptides), and "COA available on request for every product" (nzpeptide.com) — [New Zealand Peptides](https://newzealandpeptides.co.nz/shop); [NZ Peptide shop](https://nzpeptide.com/shop)
- PremiumPeps positions on **price**, undercutting New Zealand Peptides on Tirzepatide 10mg ($139.95 vs $150.00) — [PremiumPeps GLP-1 category](https://premiumpeps.co.nz/product-category/glp-1-peptides/)
- The **informal/social-media tier** is documented by 1News: a journalist obtained a Retatrutide 10mg vial for **$200 NZD** via a TikTok/Instagram "DM me" seller, with same-day residential delivery (ordered 12:45pm, delivered 1:27pm) — no formal storefront, no verifiable business identity, and a purity certificate that was six months old and of "dubious legitimacy." This $200 is markedly higher than the $165 (New Zealand Peptides) and ~$140-150 (PremiumPeps) storefront prices for the same 10mg dose — [1News: "$200 got me a vial of peptides in minutes"](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/17/200-got-me-a-vial-of-peptides-in-minutes-ai-teaches-how-to-inject-it/)
- The same social-media seller offered Tirzepatide and GHK-Cu "at similar prices," with GHK-Cu negotiable down to $160–$170 per vial depending on quantity, plus a $40 add-on for bacteriostatic water — showing informal sellers also upsell ancillary supplies — [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/17/200-got-me-a-vial-of-peptides-in-minutes-ai-teaches-how-to-inject-it/)
- RNZ reporting notes rising submissions of peptide products to NZ Drug Foundation drug-checking clinics, with products frequently differing from what was marketed — a quality signal that cuts against unverified/social-media-tier sellers specifically — [RNZ: Fears for safety as influencers push synthetic peptides](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/health/596283/fears-for-safety-as-influencers-push-synthetic-peptides)

### Inferences
- The clearest differentiator between "premium" and "budget" storefront tiers in the legitimate/quasi-legitimate NZ reseller space is **testing transparency and payment/shipping trust signals** (GST-inclusive pricing, no surcharge, COA on request) rather than large price gaps — the price spread between New Zealand Peptides and PremiumPeps on Tirzepatide 10mg is only about $10 (~7%).
- The largest and most consequential pricing/quality tier gap is not "storefront vs storefront" but **storefront vs social-media black market** — the latter charges a premium (not a discount) precisely because it trades on immediacy/convenience/anonymity rather than competing on price or verifiable quality, and it's the tier most associated with contamination and mislabeling risk.

### Gaps
- No pricing comparison could be made for "premium" claims backed by actual independent (third-party lab, e.g. Janoshik-style) COAs published publicly for Retatrutide/Tirzepatide/5-Amino-1MQ specifically — sites claim "COA available on request" but none of the fetched pages exposed an actual downloadable/viewable COA for these three compounds.
- Could not confirm whether any NZ reseller offers discreet packaging as an explicit marketed differentiator (this is common in international peptide markets but was not found stated explicitly on any NZ site fetched in this research pass).

## Are there recurring complaints about counterfeit/underdosed product, customs seizures, or price gouging in the NZ market?

### Takeaway
Yes, strongly — NZ mainstream media (RNZ, 1News, Stuff) documents a fast-growing black market with rising customs seizures, lab-testing programs that frequently find products don't match their labels, and at least one documented case (1News) of a Retatrutide purchase where the seller's COA was of "dubious legitimacy" and lab testing was inconclusive; there is also direct evidence that black-market/social-media pricing runs higher than legitimate storefronts, i.e., a form of price gouging on convenience/anonymity rather than the reverse.

### Cited Findings
- RNZ reports NZ Customs parcel seizures of suspected peptides/SARMs rose from **15 parcels in 2022 to 370 in the most recent full year, with 200 more already seized in the current year** — a roughly 25x increase over the period, indicating explosive growth in the illicit import channel — [RNZ: Fears for safety as influencers push synthetic peptides](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/health/596283/fears-for-safety-as-influencers-push-synthetic-peptides)
- Stuff reported a single seizure event of **12,000 illegal peptide drug vials worth $3 million NZD** at the NZ border, described by authorities as the "tip of the iceberg" — (full article body was not accessible during this research; this fact is drawn from the headline itself, which is a directly citable claim, but supporting detail such as which specific peptides were involved is a gap) — [Stuff: 'Tip of the iceberg': 12,000 illegal peptide drug vials worth $3m seized at NZ border](https://www.stuff.co.nz/health/361016203/tip-iceberg-12000-illegal-peptide-drug-vials-worth-3m-seized-nz-border)
- The NZ Drug Foundation reports **rising submissions of peptide products to its drug-checking clinics**, and per RNZ, testing has found that "peptides bought online weren't what they were marketed as" — direct evidence of mislabeling/counterfeiting in the NZ-accessible market — [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/health/596283/fears-for-safety-as-influencers-push-synthetic-peptides)
- Toxicology/medical commentary in the RNZ piece (Dr. Anna Skinner) warns unregulated peptides "could be contaminated with mould, heavy metals and bacteria" since they lack human trial oversight — a purity/safety risk claim, not itself proof of contamination in any specific product — [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/health/596283/fears-for-safety-as-influencers-push-synthetic-peptides)
- In the 1News investigation, a journalist's purchased "Retatrutide" 10mg vial (bought for $200 NZD from a social-media DM seller) came with a **six-month-old purity certificate (99.725%) that the report characterizes as of "dubious legitimacy,"** and NZ Drug Foundation infrared testing of the sample was **inconclusive** — unable to confirm the peptide's identity, and possibly detecting "another medication" (unconfirmed) — this is the single most concrete documented instance in this research of a specific NZ Retatrutide purchase with unverifiable/questionable contents — [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/17/200-got-me-a-vial-of-peptides-in-minutes-ai-teaches-how-to-inject-it/)
- Medsafe (per a secondary source discussing retatrutide sourcing) has warned that unapproved retatrutide products carry risks including being "linked to fatal overdose and contamination," and separately has issued a general consumer advisory on unapproved peptide products sold outside the regulated medicines system — [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/17/200-got-me-a-vial-of-peptides-in-minutes-ai-teaches-how-to-inject-it/); [NZReta secondary summary of Medsafe advisory](https://nzreta.co.nz/where-to-buy-retatrutide-nz/)
- On **price gouging specifically**: the clearest documented data point is the $200 NZD price paid to a social-media/black-market seller for a Retatrutide 10mg vial, versus $165 NZD from the New Zealand Peptides storefront and roughly the same ballpark from PremiumPeps for Tirzepatide — i.e., informal/black-market channels charged **roughly 20%+ more** than the identified legitimate NZ storefronts for a comparable dose, despite (or because of) offering no verifiable quality assurance — [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/17/200-got-me-a-vial-of-peptides-in-minutes-ai-teaches-how-to-inject-it/) vs [New Zealand Peptides](https://newzealandpeptides.co.nz/product/retatrutide/)
- No direct search results surfaced explicit community complaints (forum/Reddit posts) naming a *specific* NZ storefront reseller as guilty of underdosing or counterfeiting — the documented quality-control failures in this research trace to informal/social-media sellers, not the identified registered .co.nz/.com storefronts.

### Inferences
- The NZ regulatory/media narrative frames the "counterfeit/underdosed" risk as concentrated in the **social-media/black-market layer** (TikTok/Instagram DM sales), not primarily in the semi-formal storefront resellers (New Zealand Peptides, PremiumPeps, etc.) that this research identified — none of the storefronts had documented complaints of counterfeiting in the sources found.
- Because customs seizure volumes are rising steeply and a NZ Drug Foundation testing program exists specifically because purchased peptides often don't match labels, any NZ seller wanting to differentiate on trust should treat **publicly verifiable, batch-specific, third-party COAs** as a real competitive differentiator — the research found that even relatively well-reviewed storefronts (peptidesnz.com per Trustpilot) have customers explicitly asking for this, and it is not yet standard practice to publish it.

### Gaps
- Could not obtain the full text of the Stuff.co.nz border-seizure article, so specifics such as which peptides were in the seized 12,000 vials, when the seizure occurred, or whether Retatrutide/Tirzepatide/5-Amino-1MQ were specifically named are unknown.
- No data was found specifically quantifying underdosing (e.g., "vial contained X% of labelled dose") for Retatrutide, Tirzepatide, or 5-Amino-1MQ in the NZ market — the 1News lab test was inconclusive rather than a confirmed underdose/counterfeit finding, so this should not be overstated as proof of underdosing, only as evidence of unverifiable/questionable product.
- No community complaints (forum/Reddit/Facebook) explicitly calling out price gouging by name against a specific NZ storefront reseller were found; the price-gouging evidence in this report is inferred from comparing one journalist's documented black-market purchase price against storefront list prices, not from community sentiment directly.
