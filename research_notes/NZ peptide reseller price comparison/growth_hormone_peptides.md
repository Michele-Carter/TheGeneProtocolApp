## Is "CJC1295 without DAC + Ipamorelin" commonly sold as a pre-mixed blend by NZ sellers, or usually sold separately — and how does buying separately compare in total cost to a blended vial?

### Takeaway
Across every NZ-facing and international storefront surfaced in this research, CJC1295 (no DAC) and Ipamorelin are sold predominantly as a **pre-mixed blend/stack SKU** (often bundled further with Tesamorelin into "triple stacks"), not as two separate vials that a customer combines themselves — this appears to be the dominant industry-wide retail convention, and NZ resellers mirror it. A clean, isolated NZ-market price for a plain 2-ingredient "CJC1295 no DAC + Ipamorelin" blend (matching the user's specific 10mg blend SKU) could not be confirmed, which is a notable gap.

## Does HCG being prescription-only elsewhere affect its NZ sourcing/pricing/availability?

### Takeaway
HCG is confirmed as a legitimate prescription medicine in New Zealand for medical uses (infertility treatment, ovulation stimulation, sperm count support), which is consistent with it being tightly controlled in mainstream pharmacy channels — yet it is nonetheless readily available from the same "research chemical" RC/SARMs resellers that sell the other three peptides (Direct SARMs NZ, PharmaLabGlobal NZ both list HCG vials, pens, and nasal sprays), sold under the "for laboratory research only, not for human use" framing that lets these sites route around the prescription requirement. No direct evidence was found in this pass of HCG carrying a price premium in the NZ RC market specifically because of its prescription status elsewhere — its RC pricing appears broadly in line with (or cheaper than) the other three peptides at these same resellers.

### Cited Findings
- A search-result summary states that in the context of HCG treatment, "it is important to follow New Zealand medical advice and obtain a doctor's prescription when considering HCG treatment," and that in NZ medical practice, HCG is used for treatment of infertility in both men and women, administered to stimulate ovulation or increase sperm count — general medical-context finding, not peptide-market-specific, sourced from aggregated search summarization rather than a single identified primary NZ medical source (specific URL not resolved by the search tool).
- Despite this prescription status, Direct SARMs NZ openly lists an "HCG Peptide Vial" ($23.94–$64.08 NZD), "HCG Pre-Mixed Peptide Pen" 5000 IU ($38.83–$104.83 NZD), and "HCG Nasal Spray" ($45.60–$84.95 NZD) for sale under the research-use disclaimer — [Direct SARMs NZ HCG category](https://new-zealand.direct-sarms.com/product-category/hcg/)
- PharmaLabGlobal NZ's product-title listings likewise include an "HCG Peptide Vial," reportedly in 2000 IU or 5000 IU strengths per search-snippet summarization; no listing for a 10,000 IU size was found for this seller, and exact pricing could not be confirmed due to a 403 error on direct fetch — [PharmaLabGlobal NZ HCG Peptide Vial](https://new-zealand.pharmalabglobal.com/product/hcg-peptide-vial/)
- NZ Muscle (nzmuscle.co.nz) sells a "Biogenetic Laboratories HCG Alternative" product rather than actual HCG — consistent with mainstream NZ supplement retailers avoiding the real hormone and offering a legal alternative/support product instead, an indirect signal that genuine HCG sits outside normal retail/supplement-store channels in NZ — [NZ Muscle HCG Alternative](https://nzmuscle.co.nz/products/biogenetic-laboratories-hcg-alternative)

### Inferences
- The fact that HCG appears on the same RC storefronts (and in the same "vial/nasal spray/pre-mixed pen" product-format pattern) as the non-prescription peptides like Tesamorelin and IGF-1 LR3 suggests these NZ-facing RC resellers treat HCG no differently from the other compounds commercially — i.e., the "research chemical" framing appears to be the mechanism that lets sellers offer a prescription-controlled hormone without operating as a pharmacy, rather than HCG being notably harder to find or priced at a premium within this specific market channel.
- The existence of a legal "HCG Alternative" product at a mainstream NZ bodybuilding retailer (NZ Muscle) as opposed to real HCG supports the inference that conventional/compliant NZ retail avoids selling actual HCG, pushing genuine-HCG demand toward the RC-labeled resellers and/or grey-market/personal-import channels — but this is an inference, not a sourced statement from any retailer or forum explaining their own restocking/legal decisions.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence (forum post, reseller statement, or news article) was found explicitly stating that HCG is harder to source or import into NZ than the other three peptides, or that its prescription status drives its RC-market price up or down relative to comparable peptides. This causal question remains unanswered by available sources and is flagged as a gap rather than inferred as fact.
- No data was found on customs/import enforcement patterns specific to HCG entering NZ (e.g., seizure rates, Medsafe warnings) that would clarify whether availability is meaningfully constrained versus the other three peptides.
- Could not confirm the exact medical/regulatory source classifying HCG's legal status in NZ (e.g., a Medsafe classification datasheet) — the finding above rests on an aggregated search-summary rather than a directly fetched primary regulatory source; a follow-up query directly against a Medsafe or NZ Ministry of Health source is recommended if a citation-grade regulatory reference is needed for the final report.
