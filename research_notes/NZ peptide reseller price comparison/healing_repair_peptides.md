## Are there recurring complaints about counterfeit/underdosed BPC157/TB500 product in the NZ market?

### Takeaway
No NZ-specific consumer complaints of counterfeit or underdosed BPC-157/TB-500 product were found in this research pass. However, New Zealand's medicines regulator (Medsafe) has issued an official consumer advisory explicitly naming BPC-157 and TB-500 among unapproved peptide products at risk of contamination, incorrect strength, and non-sterile manufacture — a regulatory-level warning rather than a market-specific counterfeit complaint, but directly relevant context for purity/dosing concerns in this category.

### Cited Findings
- Medsafe (NZ's medicines safety authority) issued a "Consumer advisory: Unapproved peptide products health warning," explicitly naming **BPC-157 and TB-500** (alongside CJC-1295, GHRP-6, Ipamorelin, Melanotan II, Retatrutide, and others) as being "illegally promoted online" and warning that unapproved injectable products "have not been assessed for safety, quality, or efficacy," may "contain harmful or undisclosed ingredients," "have the wrong strength," or "be made in non-sterile, poor-quality manufacturing conditions" — [Medsafe](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/safety/Alerts/Consumer-advisory-Unapproved-peptide-products-health-warning.asp)
- The same Medsafe advisory clarifies the **legal status**: under the Medicines Act 1981 these are prescription medicines, and labels claiming "for research purposes only" or "clinically proven" are "legally meaningless" and do not authorize sale or use in people — directly relevant since these peptides are being sold/marketed under exactly this "research only" framing by every NZ seller identified above — [Medsafe](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/safety/Alerts/Consumer-advisory-Unapproved-peptide-products-health-warning.asp)
- Medsafe's regulatory classification documentation separately notes that since 2023, unscheduled BPC-157 derivatives (e.g., "NL-BPC-157 Hexadecapeptide") have been intercepted at the NZ border, and because many novel peptides are not yet formally scheduled substances, Medsafe currently lacks grounds to seize such imports at the border (products are released with a "high-risk medicine letter" instead) — [Medsafe classification agenda, via search summary](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/class/Agendas/Agen74/5.7Peptides.pdf)
- No seller-specific or product-specific complaint (e.g., "X vendor's BPC-157 was underdosed") was found for any NZ reseller in this research pass, despite multiple targeted search attempts.

### Inferences
- The absence of specific counterfeit/underdosed complaints in searchable NZ sources most likely reflects **limited public/community reporting infrastructure** for this category (no equivalent of a "Janoshik NZ vendor watch" or public COA-comparison thread was found) rather than confirmed absence of the problem — general industry knowledge (and the Medsafe advisory itself) indicates purity/dosing risk is a known category-wide issue, and BPC-157/TB-500 are widely acknowledged elsewhere (per the user's own framing) as among the most commonly counterfeited peptides internationally.
- Given that even well-reviewed NZ sellers (Peptidesnz.com) have customers explicitly asking for independent third-party purity verification rather than assuming it, a NZ seller that **proactively publishes independent COAs** (not just claims third-party testing) may have a meaningful trust/differentiation opportunity in this specific, low-transparency market.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence (forum post, review, news report) of a specific NZ BPC-157 or TB-500 batch being tested and found underdosed/counterfeit was located — this is a genuine information gap, not a confirmed "no problem exists" finding.
- Could not verify whether Medsafe, Customs NZ, or any consumer body has published batch-testing results or seizure data specific to BPC-157/TB-500 sold within NZ (as opposed to intercepted-at-border derivative compounds) — a targeted follow-up search of NZ Customs seizure reports or a Medsafe OIA-style disclosure could close this gap.
