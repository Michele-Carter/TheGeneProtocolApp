# NZ Reseller Pricing: Semax, Selank, Mots-C, SS31, NAD+, Epithalon, Cerebrolysin, Ara-290, VIP

**Reference prices being benchmarked (NZD):** Semax 5mg $70 / 10mg $90; Selank 5mg $70 / 10mg $90; Mots-C 10mg $65; SS31 10mg $85 / 50mg $180; NAD+ 500mg $80 / 1000mg $130; Epithalon 10mg $60; Cerebrolysin 60mg $80; Ara-290 10mg $90; VIP 5mg $75.

**Note on currency:** Several sites that geo-target New Zealand via a "new-zealand.*" subdomain (Direct Sarms, Direct Peptides) actually display live pricing in **GBP (£)** or **USD ($)**, not NZD, despite the NZ-branded URL. Where noted below, conversions use approximate rates of **1 GBP ≈ NZD 2.10** and **1 USD ≈ NZD 1.65** (typical September 2026 ranges) — these are approximate; no live FX tool was used, so treat converted figures as indicative, not exact.

## Which NZ-based (or NZ-shipping) resellers sell these peptides, and what do they charge per vial size?

### Takeaway
A handful of genuinely NZ-domiciled sellers (Southern Labs, NZ Peptides, NZ Peptide, Peptides New Zealand, Peptide Lab NZ) carry only a subset of the nine target peptides — mainly Semax, Selank, Mots-C, SS-31 and NAD+ — while the larger multinational storefronts that run NZ-branded subdomains (Direct Sarms, Direct Peptides, PharmaLabGlobal, PharmaGrade Store) carry the fuller range including Epithalon, Ara-290 and VIP, but bill in GBP/USD rather than NZD. Cerebrolysin could not be confirmed as stocked by any NZ-facing reseller found. Two domains that look NZ-specific (nzbuypeptides.com, nzpeptides.net) resolved to parked/inactive Hostinger pages and appear defunct despite surfacing in search results.

## Are any of these peptides noticeably harder to find from NZ sellers, suggesting a supply/demand premium?

### Takeaway
Cerebrolysin stands out as genuinely hard to source from any NZ-facing reseller checked — it did not appear on Southern Labs, NZ Peptides, Direct Sarms, Direct Peptides, or PharmaLabGlobal's NZ storefronts, despite those same sites carrying dozens of other peptides. Ara-290 and VIP, by contrast, were confirmed as live, purchasable listings (as vials, nasal sprays, or pre-mixed pens) on multiple NZ-branded storefronts, so they do not appear to be supply-constrained in the same way — availability, not absence, is the pattern for those two.

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search for Cerebrolysin across Direct Sarms, PharmaLabGlobal and Direct Peptides NZ domains returned no product listings — only tangential content (a blog-style "P-21 vs Cerebrolysin" comparison article on Direct Sarms) and unrelated peptide products (TB500, KPV, etc.) surfaced instead. — [search result set, Sept 2026]
- General (non-NZ-specific) Cerebrolysin listings exist from overseas sellers such as NuScience Peptides (30mg, "99% Purity") and Kimera Chems (5×2mL), but none of these identify as NZ-based or NZ-shipping in the material reviewed. — [NuScience Peptides](https://nusciencepeptides.com/product/cerebrolysin/), [Kimera Chems](https://kimerachems.co/product/cerebrolysin/)
- Ara-290 is confirmed as an active product on at least four NZ-facing storefronts: PharmaLabGlobal NZ (nasal spray format), Direct Sarms NZ (2mg pre-mixed pen), PharmaGrade Store (vial/nasal spray/pre-mixed pen), and Direct Peptides NZ (2mg vial, "ARA290"). — [PharmaLabGlobal ARA-290](https://new-zealand.pharmalabglobal.com/ara-290/), [Direct Sarms ARA-290 pre-mixed](https://new-zealand.direct-sarms.com/product/ara-290-pre-mixed/), [Direct Peptides ARA290](https://new-zealand.direct-peptides.com/product/ara290/)
- VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide) is confirmed as an active product on both Direct Sarms NZ (2mg vial, $22.76–$33.99 USD) and Direct Peptides NZ (vial, product-category page live), i.e., it is not a hard-to-find item among the international-template storefronts. — [Direct Sarms VIP vial](https://new-zealand.direct-sarms.com/product/vip-peptide-vial), [Direct Peptides VIP category](https://new-zealand.direct-peptides.com/product-category/vip/)
- None of the genuinely NZ-domestic-stock sellers found (Southern Labs) list NAD+, Epithalon, Cerebrolysin, Ara-290, or VIP at all — their catalogue centers on Semax, Selank, Mots-C, SS-31, BPC-157, TB-500, GLP-1 analogues, and similar "mainstream" research-peptide demand. — [Southern Labs](https://southernlabs.co.nz/)

### Inferences
- Cerebrolysin's absence likely reflects genuine supply/demand economics distinct from the others: it's a complex, non-single-sequence neuropeptide extract (porcine-brain-derived enzymatic hydrolysate) rather than a synthesized single peptide, which plausibly makes it costlier/harder for small resellers to source, store (it typically requires cold-chain / different handling than lyophilized synthetic peptides), and verify by COA — a structurally different sourcing problem than for Semax or Selank. This is an inference, not a confirmed fact from any source reviewed.
- The fact that Ara-290 and VIP are stocked at the large international-template stores but not at the smaller genuinely-domestic NZ sellers (Southern Labs) suggests NZ retail demand for these two is currently thinner/more niche than for Semax/Selank/BPC-157-type products — domestic sellers stock to observed local demand, and haven't yet added Ara-290/VIP to their range, while the bigger multi-country operations carry a long tail regardless of any single country's demand.

### Gaps
- No pricing data at all (NZD or otherwise) could be found for Cerebrolysin from an NZ-facing seller, which prevents any direct price-premium comparison against the reference $80/60mg price — this gap is itself the key evidence for a potential premium/scarcity, but it cannot be quantified without finding an actual NZ (or NZ-shipping) listing.
- Could not confirm whether Cerebrolysin is entirely unavailable to NZ buyers or simply not carried by the specific sites sampled — a wider sweep (e.g., of the "Top 29 Peptide Manufacturers in New Zealand" ensun.io directory, or direct outreach/contact-form queries to sellers) would be needed to rule it out completely.

## How does NAD+ clinic-administered pricing compare to raw vial pricing?

### Takeaway
NAD+ delivered via NZ wellness/IV-therapy clinics carries a very large price premium per milligram compared to raw research-vial pricing: one confirmed in-clinic NAD+ session price works out to roughly NZD $3.50/mg, versus raw vial pricing implied by research-chemical resellers that is more in the range of $0.10–$0.45/mg (using the reference $80/500mg ≈ $0.16/mg, and Direct Sarms' converted $83–$449 NZD for 250–500mg vials/kits, i.e., roughly $0.33–$0.90/mg before the higher end which includes value-pack/kit premiums).

### Cited Findings
- **Clinic 1 (Auckland & Christchurch), "VAION NAD+"** — In-clinic SubQ injection: **NZ$350 per session**, described as "2 small SubQ injections of 50mg spaced 5–10 minutes apart" (i.e., 100mg delivered per $350 session ≈ NZD $3.50/mg). At-home SubQ pen option: **NZ$1,250 for a 40-day supply** (~20 doses every other day); total mg per pen not stated on the page fetched, so a per-mg figure for the at-home option could not be calculated. — [Clinic 1 VAION NAD+](https://www.clinic1.co.nz/vaion-nad)
- Other NZ clinics confirmed to offer NAD+ IV/injection services, though specific NAD+ pricing was not captured (either not published or would require direct contact): Drips NZ (mobile IV/longevity service, Auckland-based; pricing page did not list a dollar figure for NAD+ specifically and directs enquiries to email/phone), Aqua Viva (Parnell Health Hub, described as an early/experienced NZ IV nutrient therapy provider since the early 2000s), Renew Wellness Place (Auckland, general IV infusions advertised "from $249 per infusion" — not NAD+-specific), P3 Recovery, NAHAW (Wellington), Revive, and The Wellness Nurse. — [Drips NZ](https://drips.nz/journal/nad-the-essential-molecule-for-cellular-health-and-longevity), [Renew Wellness Place](https://renewwellnessplace.com/partners/iv-therapy)
- Raw research-vial NAD+ pricing found: Direct Sarms NZ subdomain lists NAD+ vials at 250mg/500mg (plus value-pack/kit variants) spanning **$50.00–$271.93 USD** (≈ NZD $83–$449 at ~1.65 conversion); Peptides New Zealand lists an NAD+ 500mg vial at **$99.99** (currency unconfirmed as NZD); the user's own reference pricing is NZD $80/500mg and $130/1000mg (≈ $0.16–$0.13/mg). — [Direct Sarms NAD+ vial](https://new-zealand.direct-sarms.com/product/nad-vial)

### Inferences
- Even using the most conservative (lowest) clinic price found (NZ$350 for 100mg = $3.50/mg) against the highest raw-vial per-mg cost found (Direct Sarms' upper-end kit pricing, roughly $0.90/mg), clinic-administered NAD+ carries at least a ~4x per-mg premium over raw vial product, and against the reference price list's $0.16/mg it is closer to a 20x premium — consistent with the expected pattern that clinic pricing bundles a professional administration fee, nursing/practitioner time, and a finished/sterile-for-injection product, none of which raw research-vial pricing includes.
- This gap is useful context for a NZ research-peptide seller: it indicates real willingness-to-pay for NAD+ delivery exists at a much higher price point in the clinic channel, which may support premium-tier or "ready-to-use" NAD+ product positioning if the seller wanted to test it, though doing so would cross from a research-chemical framing into a therapeutic/administration framing with different regulatory considerations.

### Gaps
- Could not obtain a specific NZD price for NAD+ at Drips NZ, Aqua Viva, P3 Recovery, NAHAW, Revive, or The Wellness Nurse — these would need direct-contact follow-up (email/phone) since pricing is not published on the pages fetched.
- Could not confirm currency for the Peptides New Zealand $99.99 NAD+ vial price (site fetch failed; figure is from a search-engine snippet only).
- No data was found on typical dosing/mg-per-session at Drips NZ or Aqua Viva to allow a second independent per-mg clinic comparison beyond the single Clinic 1 data point.
